# DITHAR (دِثار) — Adhan Audio Licensing Research

_Research date: 2026-10-09. All findings come from live web sources accessed on that date. Licence terms change, so re-check every link before you sign or ship anything._

> **This is not legal advice.** It is evidence-gathering to prepare for a decision. Have a lawyer review the final agreement(s) before you publish, especially for anything involving Saudi, Kuwaiti/Malaysian or Egyptian rights holders.

> **Research direction updated 2026-10-09.** The primary track is now existing, openly licensed recordings. See **[Open Audio Archives — Deep Search](#open-audio-archives--deep-search)** at the end of this document. Sections A–G (licensing and commissioning) remain as background and fallback.

## Research instructions — Mandatory Open-Source Fallback (standing rule, added 2026-10-09)

If permission for a famous Egyptian or Gulf muadhin's adhan cannot be obtained, **don't drop that voice.** Search extensively for an existing recording of the **same** muadhin in any legitimate open source:
- GitHub repositories containing actual audio files
- Internet Archive and other digital audio archives
- Wikimedia Commons and other openly licensed media repositories
- public university, library, mosque, radio and TV archives
- other legitimate public repositories with downloadable audio and verifiable licensing

Search by the muadhin's name in Arabic and English, alternate spellings, mosque names, recording titles and archive metadata. Prioritise the classical Egyptian voices and the Gulf voices.

**For every matching recording, verify:**
1. It is genuinely an **adhan** by the named muadhin, not Qur'an recitation.
2. The original recording source and uploader.
3. The exact licence attached to that particular recording.
4. Whether commercial mobile-app redistribution, offline playback and the necessary audio processing are allowed.
5. Whether the uploader has credible authority to grant those rights.

Public-domain, CC0 or suitably licensed Creative Commons recordings can be candidates, but verify the evidence and terms **per file**. A code repository's software licence is **not** an audio licence. An archive upload, a YouTube download or a CC tag does **not** by itself prove the uploader owns the recording.

**Decision process for every desired famous voice:**
- **A.** Search for a recording with documented, compatible reuse rights.
- **B.** If none is found, search alternative archives and alternate versions of the same adhan.
- **C.** If nothing suitable turns up after a thorough search, mark it **"No verified open recording found"** and record what was searched.
- **D.** Move on to the remaining voices. Never replace a famous voice with an unknown performer without clearly separating the two.

No contacting anyone, no purchasing, no app-code changes. Results: **[Famous Voices — Open-Source Fallback Search](#famous-voices--open-source-fallback-search)**.

## How classifications are used

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **VERIFIED** | Explicit licence terms or written permission from the **rights holder** cover bundling the file in a mobile app, commercial use and offline playback. |
| **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** | Credible source with some explicit terms, but at least one required right (commercial use, chain of title, performer consent, app-sound use) is unclear. |
| **UNVERIFIED** | No adequate evidence that the required rights exist or have been granted. |

A sound recording carries **two layers of rights**: (1) copyright in the recording (master), usually held by whoever made or commissioned it, and (2) the **performer's rights** of the muadhin. The adhan text itself is not copyrightable. A licence is only as good as the licensor's own title, so permission from a website that does not own the recording is **not** a licence.

## A. Executive recommendation

**The shortest realistic path to documented rights is to commission a new recording under a written agreement (Option 1). Use one carefully chosen openly-licensed or stock file only as a temporary placeholder.**

Why:

1. **None of the famous muadhins' recordings could be verified as licensable.** Ali Ahmed Mulla, the Haramain broadcasts, Alafasy, Al-Qatami and Abdul Basit have no published licence or price. Their recordings are controlled by (or likely controlled by) government broadcasters, mosque authorities or private companies whose terms are restrictive or unpublished. Every route needs a custom negotiation with an uncertain outcome.
2. **The one aggregator that seems to "allow" bundling does not own the files.** Islamic Network (AlAdhan.com) told one developer bundling was fine, but its own terms say copyrights "lie with the reciters and they may ask you to remove the content". Its redistribution right covers **non-commercial** use only. That is not adequate for a commercial-capable app.
3. **Openly licensed recordings exist but have weak chain of title.** On Wikimedia Commons and Freesound the licences are explicit, but the uploads are self-declared "own work" with no verified permission from the performer. One CC0 "adhan" is a blocked sockpuppet's upload. Another "CC0" Freesound file openly says it was ripped from YouTube, so it is invalid.
4. **Stock audio is cheap with clear terms, but it is the wrong product.** The best candidates are Istanbul street recordings ($2–$3). Envato's licence expressly bars distributing an item "as a ringtone" or letting users extract it. An adhan alert sound sits close to that line.
5. **A commissioned recording solves every unknown at once.** You get one qualified muadhin, a studio-quality file and a signed agreement. The agreement assigns copyright (or grants an exclusive, perpetual, worldwide licence), includes the performer's consent to app use, allows editing, and has no royalties. Muslim Pro uses exactly this model: its acknowledgements state that its adhan "by Issam Bayan is licensed by Issam Bayan".

**Secondary strategic track (optional, slower):** ask the **Saudi Broadcasting Authority's archive-licensing portal, Sayma** ([sayma.sba.sa](https://sayma.sba.sa/)) for a quote on a Haramain adhan. This is the most plausible official route to a "famous" Makkah adhan, but whether the catalogue includes adhan audio, the terms and the price are all **unverified**.

## E. Recommended action plan (priority order)

1. **Decide the spec** (internal, today). Pick a style (Makkah/Hijazi, Madinah, Egyptian, Turkish), the variants needed (standard adhan; Fajr with *aṣ-ṣalātu khayrun mina n-nawm*; optionally a short notification version), and the delivery format: 48 kHz/24-bit WAV masters plus app-ready AAC/Opus.
2. **Start commissioning (Option 1).** Shortlist 3–5 qualified muadhins or Islamic audio studios: your local mosque network, Islamic production houses, or vetted freelance voice artists. Request written quotes. Contact only when you decide to, using the template in section F (adapted).
3. **Draft a recording and rights agreement** (lawyer-reviewed) containing:
   - assignment of copyright in the recording to DITHAR, or an exclusive, perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free licence;
   - the performer's consent and waiver for use of their voice and performance in apps, streaming, downloads and offline playback;
   - the right to edit, trim, normalise, transcode and create short notification versions;
   - the right to sublicense to app stores, CDNs and distributors as needed;
   - attribution preference (name credit or anonymous);
   - a warranty that the performer is free to grant these rights (no exclusive deal elsewhere);
   - written permission from the mosque if recorded on its premises.
4. **Interim placeholder (optional).** If you need audio before the commission is delivered, the least-risky interim choice is **Wikimedia "The Adhan – Aaqib Azeez" (CC BY-SA 4.0)**, with full attribution, used unmodified or with the ShareAlike consequences understood. Document the licence page with a dated screenshot or PDF. Replace it once the commissioned file arrives. Alternatively, buy **AudioJungle #52260962 ($2)**, but only if you are comfortable with the ringtone/extraction clause. Package files so users cannot export them.
5. **Strategic track: famous Makkah adhan.** Create an account on [Sayma](https://sayma.sba.sa/) and search for Haram adhan assets. If present, send the SBA inquiry from section F. In parallel, ask the Haramain authority (info@gph.gov.sa) who owns the Haram adhan audio.
6. **Optional: Alafasy.** Send the section F inquiry to Alafasy Berhad, naming the exact recordings. Expect a slow response or a refusal.
7. **Before release:** add an "Audio credits & licences" screen, store signed agreements and licence snapshots in a secure folder (not the public repo), and keep files bundled so users cannot extract them.

## G. Unresolved questions (must be answered before publishing audio)

1. **Chain of title:** For each open-licensed file, is the uploader the person who made the recording, and did the muadhin consent? (None of the Wikimedia files have a VRT permission ticket.)
2. **Performer's rights:** Does the chosen jurisdiction(s) give the muadhin separate performer's rights that a recording licence doesn't cover? This needs the performer's consent in writing.
3. **Alert-sound use vs. "ringtone" restrictions:** Does using a stock adhan as the app's prayer-time notification count as distributing it "as a ringtone" under Envato's licence? Ask Envato support before relying on it.
4. **Extraction:** On Android/iOS, notification sounds must sometimes be copied into accessible storage (e.g. Android `res/raw` or the iOS app bundle). Is that technically "permitting an end user to extract the item"? Confirm with your lawyer or the licensor.
5. **ShareAlike scope (CC BY-SA):** If you trim or normalise a CC BY-SA file, the adapted audio must stay under CC BY-SA. Confirm it does not affect the rest of the app (generally it does not, but get it checked).
6. **Haramain ownership:** Who owns Haram adhan audio: SBA, the General Authority for the Two Holy Mosques, or both? Is adhan audio offered on Sayma at all, and on what terms?
7. **Alafasy catalogue:** Which Alafasy adhan recordings does Alafasy Berhad actually own, as opposed to earlier labels? Would it license to a competing prayer app?
8. **Issam Bayan:** Is the Muslim Pro licence exclusive? Is there a contact channel?
9. **Religious and brand sensitivity:** Envato's licence bars use "in connection with sensitive subjects". Confirm that a religious app is acceptable under that FAQ.
10. **Commission contract law:** Governing law and enforceability for a cross-border agreement with a muadhin in another country. Also tax and withholding on payments.

## Open Audio Archives — Deep Search

_Searched 2026-10-09: GitHub (repository and code search), Internet Archive (advanced search plus item metadata API), Wikimedia Commons (category tree and media search), Freesound and Europeana. Every audio file listed with technical figures was downloaded and measured with `ffprobe`/`ffmpeg`. Audio was **not** listened to, so every recommendation below still needs a human listening check for correct wording, maqam and recitation quality._

### Method and rules applied

- **A repository's software licence was never treated as covering its audio.** Example: `elpasha3000/AdhanBox` is MIT-licensed and says "redistribute it… no permission needed", but it bundles `makkah-adhan.mp3`, a Saudi state broadcast. The MIT licence cannot grant rights the author never held.
- **"Own work" is the uploader's claim, not proof.** Where a performer other than the uploader is audible, the performer's consent is undocumented unless stated otherwise.
- **The Public Domain Mark (PDM) is a label, not a dedication.** It asserts that a work is *already* public domain. On a 2010s recording it is either a mistake or a disguised waiver. Its value depends entirely on who applied it.
- **Quality figures are indicative.** Integrated loudness (LUFS) and "dynamic range" (RMS peak minus RMS trough in 0.4 s windows) come from ffmpeg. A wide range (40 dB or more) usually means a clean close-mic recording. A narrow range (under 25 dB) usually means a noisy, ambient or crowd recording.

| Class | Meaning |

| **A — Strong candidate** | Explicit licence evidence from a party with a credible claim to the rights supports redistribution, commercial use and app bundling. |
| **B — Needs verification** | Public and openly labelled, but ownership, performer consent or completeness is unclear. |
| **C — Unsuitable** | Non-commercial or no-derivatives terms, no licence at all, or credible evidence that the uploader lacked the rights. |

### Licence obligations if you adopt the top picks

- **CC BY 4.0** (Developer Athan 1/2/3): credit "Adhan recording by Ahmed Khalaf, CC BY 4.0", with links to the licence and the source repo, and state "loudness-normalised/trimmed" if you edit. **Effective-technological-measures clause (§2(a)(5)(C)):** don't stop recipients exercising the licence. Keeping the file in an app bundle is generally acceptable, but linking to the original source file in your credits screen removes doubt.
- **CC BY 3.0** (Malmö): credit "Islamic Center Malmö", with links to the licence and the original YouTube video, and a note that audio was extracted and edited.
- **CC BY-SA 4.0** (Aaqib Azeez, Azan.ogg, etc.): the same attribution, **plus** any trimmed or normalised version of the audio must itself be offered under CC BY-SA 4.0. This applies only to the audio, not your app.
- **Keep evidence:** save dated PDFs or snapshots of each licence page and the Athan `LICENSE` at commit `821da16`, plus the SHA-1 of each file you ship.

### Open questions specific to this track

1. **Athan repo:** is Ahmed Khalaf the voice on `Developer_Athan-*`, or only the recordist? (Answering this would require asking him. Not done, per instructions.)
2. **Malmö:** who is the muadhin, and does the mosque's CC BY grant reflect his consent? A mosque publishing its own recording is strong but not conclusive.
3. **Makkah videos (#10, #11):** does a visitor's CC BY licence on a recording of the Haram PA hold up against the muadhin's performer's rights and the Haram authority's filming rules? Treat both as **not cleared**.
4. **Completeness:** confirm by ear that each chosen file is a complete, correctly worded adhan. The short files (42 s, 37 s, 1:27) may be partial.

Three recordings are **Class A**: Developer Athan 1, Developer Athan 3 (Fajr), and the Malmö Fajr adhan. Developer Athan 2 is also A, but quiet. Together they give DITHAR a lawful standard adhan plus two Fajr options with **no fee, no negotiation and no non-commercial restriction**, at the cost of attribution only. **None of them is by a famous muadhin.** No openly licensed recording by Ali Ahmed Mulla, Alafasy, Al-Qatami or Abdul Basit, and no Haramain broadcast, could be verified anywhere searched. ### Three different questions

| Tier | Meaning | What establishes it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Technically downloadable** | The file can be fetched from a public URL. | A working link. **Says nothing about rights.** |
| **2. Openly licensed** | The page or repository carries a licence that permits commercial redistribution, offline use and editing. | The licence tag or text. **Only as good as the licensor's own title.** |
| **3. Sufficiently rights-cleared for release** | Tier 2, **plus** documented evidence that the licensor controls the recording **and** the performer consented. Also requires that DITHAR can meet every licence condition. | Licensor identity, performer link, licence history, integrity check, and an attribution plan. **Still not a legal opinion.** |

### Key structural finding

Most "free famous adhan" files on GitHub and Internet Archive trace back to three non-open sources: IslamWeb (non-commercial), assabile.com, and broadcast captures. The only famous-voice recordings with a credible open licence are **CC BY videos posted on the performer's or rights-holder's own official YouTube channel**. Those channels set licences **per video**: most of their uploads are "Standard YouTube License", and a few are deliberately marked CC BY. That pattern makes the CC BY choice look deliberate rather than a default.

> YouTube's CC BY option ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797468)) may only be applied to "your originally created content", CC BY content or public-domain content, and **cannot** be applied while a Content ID claim exists. The help page now links CC BY 4.0. Older uploads were licensed under CC BY 3.0 Unported, as Commons records for the Malmö video. Confirm which version applied at each video's upload date.

### Old Egyptian recordings and public domain: why age doesn't solve it

- **Egypt:** sound-recording producers' rights last 50 years from recording or publication ([Commons: Egypt](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Egypt), Law 82/2002). Pre-1975 recordings may therefore be out of producer protection in Egypt. _(Added on resume.)_ Two points remain unverified. First, the **performer's** term: it is commonly reported as 50 years, but the article could not be checked against an official text this session (WIPO Lex lookup failed). Second, whether Law 82/2002 reaches recordings made under the 1954 law at all. Dating anchor for Rifaat: he stopped reciting after a 1943 throat illness and died in 1950 ([Diwan al-Arab](https://www.diwanalarab.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D9%8A%D8%AE-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%AF-%D8%B1%D9%81%D8%B9%D8%AA)), so any Rifaat adhan dates from 1934–c.1950. On the US rule below, that means protection until about 2034–2060.
- **United States (App Store/Play distribution):** pre-1972 sound recordings are federally protected under the Music Modernization Act. Recordings first published 1923–1946 are protected for 100 years, 1947–1956 for 110 years, and 1957–1972 until 2067 ([U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/music-modernization/pre1972-soundrecordings/)). A 1940s Rifaat or 1960s Abdul Basit recording is **not** public domain in the US on that basis. How this applies to foreign recordings, and to recordings never formally "published", needs counsel.
- **Practical consequence:** none of the classical Egyptian adhans can be treated as public domain worldwide. The **CC BY grant on the official channel** is the only open route found, and it depends on that channel's authority.

### Obtaining the audio: an open issue

The CC BY licence permits reuse, but YouTube's Terms of Service restrict downloading content except through features YouTube provides. Wikimedia Commons routinely imports CC BY YouTube videos (as with the Malmö file), but whether a commercial app may obtain the file that way needs legal review. The cleaner route is to ask each channel for the source file. That requires contact, which this research did not do. **No evaluation copies of YouTube items were downloaded.**
